## P293 T5

Let $R = Z[\sqrt{-n}]$ where $n$ is a squarefree integer greater than 3.

a. Prove that $2$, $\sqrt{-n}$ and $1 + \sqrt{-n}$ are irreducibles in $R$.

b. Prove that $R$ is not a U.F.D. Conclude that the quadratic integer ring $\mathcal O$ is not a U.F.D. for $D \equiv 2, 3 \bmod 4$, $D < -3$ (so also not Euclidean and not a P.I.D.).

c. Give an explicit ideal in $R$ that is not principal.

[Using (b) consider a maximal ideal containing the nonprime ideal (Fn) or (1 + Fn).]

---

a. We have $(a+b\sqrt{-n})(c+d\sqrt{-n})=(ac-nbd)+(ad+bc)\sqrt{-n}$.

Suppose $ad+bc=0$, $ac-nbd=1$. Then $bc=-ad$, $abc-nb^2d=b$, $-a^2d-nb^2d=b$, $b=-d(a^2+nb^2)$. Compare $N(b)$ and $N(a^2+nb^2)$, we have $b=0$. Thus $ac=1$, and $a=\plusmn 1$. So the only unit is $\plusmn 1$.

Likewise, if $ad+bc=0$, $ac-nbd=2$, also we have $b=0$, then $ac=2$, so $a=\plusmn 1$ or $a=\plusmn 2$. Thus the only reduce is $2=\plusmn 1*\plusmn 2$, thus $2$ is irreducible.

Suppose $ad+bc=1$, $ac-nbd=0$. Likewise, we have $a=d(a^2+nb^2)$, then $a=0$ or $a=\plusmn 1$. Thus the only reduce is $\sqrt{-n}=\plusmn 1*\plusmn \sqrt{-n}$, thus $\sqrt{-n}$ is irreducible.

Suppose $ad+bc=1$, $ac-nbd=1$. Likewise, we have $(a-b)=d(a^2+nb^2)$, then $a=b$ or $b=0$. Thus the only reduce is $1+\sqrt{-n}=\plusmn 1*\plusmn (1+\sqrt{-n})$.

b. We have $(1+\sqrt{-n})(1-\sqrt{-n})=1+n^2$, $(2+\sqrt{-n})(2-\sqrt{-n})=4+n^2$, so one of them is dividable of $2$. But since the coefficient of $\sqrt{-n}$ is $\plusmn 1$ and not dividable of $2$, thus $2$ is not prime. But $2$ is irreducible, then $R$ is not UFD.

c. Suppose $(2, 1+\sqrt{-n})=(m)$, then $2=rm$. Since $2$ is irreducible, if $m$ is unit, then $(2, 1+\sqrt{-n})=Z[\sqrt{-n}]$; if $r$ is unit, then $(2, 1+\sqrt{-n})=(2)$.

But we have $1+\sqrt{-n} \notin (2)$, so $(2, 1+\sqrt{-n})=Z[\sqrt{-n}]$. That is $2(a+b\sqrt{-n})+(1+\sqrt{-n})(c+d\sqrt{-n})=1$, that is $2a+c-nd=1$, $2b+d+c=0$. If $n$ is odd, then $c+d$ should be both odd and even by the two equation. So $n$ must be even.

Likewise, consider $(2, \sqrt{-n})=(m')$, we can deduce $n$ must be odd, thus $(2, 1+\sqrt{-n})$ or $(2, \sqrt{-n})$ is such a ideal.

## P294 T11

Prove that $R$ is a P.I.D. if and only if $R$ is a U.F.D. that is also a Bezout Domain.

An integral domain $R$ in which every ideal generated by two elements is principal (i.e., for every $a, b \in R$, $(a, b) = (d)$ for some $d \in R$) is called a Bezout Domain.

---

Only if: Obvious. If: Let $I$ be a ideal in $R$, and let $x \in I$. If $x=up_1^{c_1} \cdots p_n^{c_n}$, let set $S_x=\{p_i^j \mid 1 \le j \le c_i\}$, let $S=\cap_{x \in I} S_x$. Let $g \in R$ be the element correspond to $S$, we have $g \in I$ since $\gcd(a, b) \in (a, b)$.

We claim $(g)=I$. If $(g) \subset I$, then $y \notin (g)$ but $y \in I$. We have $(g, y) = (z) \subseteq I$, then $S \subseteq S_z$, thus $g \mid z$ and $(g, y)=(z)=(g)$, contradiction.

## P298 T13

Prove that the rings $F[x, y]/(y^2 - x)$ and $F[x, y]/(y^2 - x^2)$ are not isomorphic for any field $F$.

---

We have $[(y+x)+(y^2-x^2)][(y-x)+(y^2-x^2)]=0+(y^2-x^2)$, so $F[x, y]/(y^2 - x^2)$ has nonzero zero divisor.

Suppose $[a+(y^2-x)][b+(y^2-x)]=0+(y^2-x)$, thus $y^2-x \mid ab$. We claim that $y^2-x$ is prime, then $a \in (y^2-x)$ or $b \in (y^2-x)$, thus $F[x, y]/(y^2 - x)$ has no nonzero zero divisor.

To prove $y^2-x$ is prime, we have $F[x, y]$ is ED thus UFD, so we only prove $y^2-x$ is irreducible.

If $y^2-x=ab$, then $\deg(a)+\deg(b)=2$, and $a, b$ is not unit thus $\deg(a), \deg(b)>0$. So $\deg(a)=\deg(b)=1$, $a=ix+jy$, $b=kx+ly$. We have $ik=0$, since $F$ is field, we have $i=0$ or $k=0$. Suppose $k=0$, then $y \mid ab$. But $y \nmid y^2-x$. Contradiction.

## P306 T4

Let $R = Z+xQ[x] \subset Q[x]$ be the set of polynomials in $x$ with rational coefficients whose constant term is an integer.

a. Prove that $R$ is an integral domain and its units are $\plusmn 1$.

b. Show that the irreducibles in $R$ are $\plusmn p$ where $p$ is a prime in $Z$ and the polynomials $f(x)$ that are irreducible in $Q[x]$ and have constant term $\plusmn 1$. Prove that these irreducibles are prime in $R$.

c. Show that $x$ cannot be written as the product of irreducibles in $R$ (in particular, $x$ is not irreducible) and conclude that $R$ is not a U.F.D.

d. Show that $x$ is not a prime in $R$ and describe the quotient ring $R/(x)$.

---

a. Since $Q[x]$ is integral, then $R$ is integral. Since unit of $Q[x]$ is only $Q \setminus \{0\}$, unit of $R$ is in $(Q \setminus \{0\}) \cap R = Z \setminus \{0\}$. If $n \in Z$ is a unit in $R$, then $n$ is a unit in $Z$, so the unit is only $\plusmn 1$.

b. For arbitary $f(x) \in R$. If $\deg(f)=0$, then $f$ is irreducible iff $f_0$ is irreducible in $Z$, that is $f_0$ is prime.

If $\deg(f)>0$. If $f_0=0$, then $f=2 \cdot (f/2)$; if $|f_0|>1$, then $f = f_0 \cdot (f/f_0)$. So $f$ irreducible deduce $f_0 = \plusmn 1$. If $f$ is reducible in $Q[x]$, then $f(x)=a(x)b(x)$, and $f_0=a_0b_0$. Let $a'(x)=a(x)f_0/a_0$, $b'(x)=b(x)/b_0$, then $a'(x), b'(x) \in R$ and is not unit. So $f$ is reducible in $R$. Thus $f$ irreducible deduce $f_0 = \plusmn 1$ and irreducible in $Q[x]$.

If $f_0 = \plusmn 1$ and irreducible in $Q[x]$, then $f(x)=a(x)b(x)$ deduce that $a_0b_0=\plusmn 1$, $\deg(a) = 0$ or $\deg(b) = 0$. That is $a_0 = \plusmn 1$, $b_0 = \plusmn 1$. So $a$ or $b$ is unit. Thus $f$ is irreducible in $R$.

c. If $x=f_1f_2 \cdots f_n$, then suppose $\deg(f_1)=1$, $\deg(f_i)=0$ for $i>1$. Thus let $f=f_1$, $g=f_2 \cdots f_n$, we have $\deg(f)=1$, $\deg(g)=0$. So we have $g_0$ is prime and $f_0 = \plusmn 1$. But we need $f_0g_0=0$, so $g_0 = 0$. Then $g=0$, $x=fg=0$, contradiction.

d. We have $(x)=xZ+x^2Q[x]$. Consider $\frac{1}{4}x^2 \in (x)$, $x^2=(\frac{1}{2}x)^2$, but $\frac{1}{2}x \notin (x)$. So $x$ is not prime.

Let $\varphi : R \rarr Z \times (Q/Z)$ that $\varphi(a+\frac{b}{c}x+\cdots) = (a, \frac{b}{c}+Z)$, then $\varphi$ is natural projection, $\ker \varphi = xZ+x^2Q[x]=(x)$. So $R/(x) \cong Z \times (Q/Z)$.
